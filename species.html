Species Guide

Below is a selection of birds commonly found in Philadelphia throughout the year. This is not a complete list; Philadelphia and the surrounding region is home to hundreds of species, particularly during migration season. For a more comprehensive reference, please visit our Resources page, where you will find links to eBird, Merlin, and other identification tools.

Illustrations are from John James Audubon's Birds of America and are provided for historical reference.

Year-Round Birds

These birds can be found in Philadelphia during any season.

Northern Cardinal

One of the most recognizable birds in North America. Males are brilliant red with a black mask; females are warm brown with reddish accents. Both sexes have a distinctive crest. Cardinals are common in woodland edges, gardens, and backyards across Philadelphia and are frequent feeder visitors.

Blue Jay

A large, bold songbird with bright blue, black, and white plumage. Blue Jays are intelligent, social, and loud — their calls are hard to miss. They are found throughout Philadelphia in parks, woodlands, and suburban neighborhoods year-round.

Downy Woodpecker

The smallest woodpecker in North America and one of the most common. Downy Woodpeckers are black and white with a small red spot on the back of the male's head. They are frequently seen clinging to tree trunks and branches in parks and backyards throughout the city.

American Crow

Large, all-black, and highly intelligent. American Crows are found throughout Philadelphia in nearly every habitat — parks, neighborhoods, parking lots, and open fields. Their loud, familiar "caw" is one of the most recognizable bird calls in the city.

Belted Kingfisher

A stocky, large-headed bird with a shaggy crest and a distinctive rattling call. Belted Kingfishers are found near water — along Wissahickon Creek, Pennypack Creek, and at John Heinz and FDR Park. Watch for them perching over water before diving to catch fish.

Spring & Summer Birds

These birds can be found in Philadelphia during the Spring & Summer.

Baltimore Oriole

A striking bird — males are brilliant orange and black, females are yellowish-olive. Baltimore Orioles arrive in Philadelphia in late April and are found in tall deciduous trees in parks and neighborhoods. Their rich, flute-like song is one of the sounds of a Philadelphia spring.

Ruby-throated Hummingbird

The only hummingbird regularly found in eastern North America. Males have a brilliant iridescent red throat; females are green and white. Ruby-throated Hummingbirds arrive in Philadelphia in May and are attracted to tubular flowers and nectar feeders. They depart for the tropics in early fall.

Fall & Winter Birds

These birds can be found passing through or spending time in Philadelphia during the Fall & Winter.

American Goldfinch

Males in breeding plumage are brilliant yellow with black wings; in winter, both sexes are a more muted olive-yellow. Goldfinches are common at feeders throughout Philadelphia and are found in open fields and woodland edges. They are particularly fond of thistle and sunflower seeds.

Dark-eyed Junco

A small sparrow-like bird with dark gray or brown plumage and a white belly. Dark-eyed Juncos are common winter visitors to Philadelphia, arriving in October and departing by April. They are frequently seen hopping on the ground beneath feeders and in woodland edges, foraging for seeds. They are colloquially known as Snowbirds!

Migration Birds

Philadelphia's position on the Atlantic Flyway makes it an excellent place to observe migratory species.

Great Blue Heron

The largest heron in North America, standing up to four feet tall. Great Blue Herons are found near water throughout Philadelphia — along creeks, at FDR Park's lagoons, and at John Heinz. They stand motionless for long periods before striking at fish with their long bills. A reliable and impressive bird for beginners to find.

Osprey

A large fish-hunting raptor with distinctive brown and white plumage and a dark eye stripe. Ospreys pass through Philadelphia during spring and fall migration and are regularly seen at John Heinz and FDR Park. Watch for them hovering over water before plunging feet-first to catch fish.

For a full list of species recorded in Philadelphia County, visit eBird Philadelphia County. To identify birds you see in the field, the Merlin Bird ID app is free and highly recommended.
