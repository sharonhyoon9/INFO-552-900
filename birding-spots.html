Birding Spots

Philadelphia has over a hundred parks and green spaces, many of which offer excellent birding. Below are some of the best and most accessible spots within Philadelphia County. Whether you are looking for a quick outing close to home or a longer walk through dense woodland, there is something here for every level.

Note that you do not have to venture to green spaces to bird! Various birds can be spotted within the urban cityscape of Philadelphia as well.

John Heinz National Wildlife Refuge at Tinicum

Address: 8601 Lindbergh Blvd, Philadelphia, PA 19153

Known as America's first urban wildlife refuge, John Heinz is widely considered the best birding spot in Philadelphia. The refuge supports over 300 bird species across its wetland and woodland habitats. The freshwater tidal marsh attracts migratory waterfowl, shorebirds, herons, and marsh birds including the Marsh Wren and Least Bittern. The surrounding woodlands are excellent for migratory songbirds. The refuge features 10 miles of trails and a visitor center that offers birding checklists and free binocular loans.

Best for: Waterfowl, shorebirds, wading birds, songbirds during migration

FDR Park

Address: 1500 Pattison Ave, Philadelphia, PA 19145

FDR Park is one of Philadelphia's top birding locations. The 348-acre park contains one of the most diverse bird habitats in the city, with fields, ponds, lakes, and lagoons that have recorded over 200 species. Located on the Atlantic coastal plain, it is especially good for waterfowl and is a regular stop for organized birding walks run by BirdPhilly. Note that parts of the park are prone to flooding; as such, waterproof shoes are recommended.

Best for: Waterfowl, wading birds, meadow species

Wissahickon Valley Park

Address: Accessible from multiple entry points; main access via Valley Green Road

Wissahickon Valley Park is one of the largest urban forests in the United States and one of Philadelphia's premier birding locations. The park's creeks, dense woodland, and varied terrain support a wide range of species year-round. It is particularly excellent during spring migration. BirdPhilly runs "Migration Madness" walks at the Park on Fridays from late April through May. The Wissahickon Creek Loop is also a popular birding trail, running nearly 6 miles.

Best for: Forest songbirds, warblers during migration, year-round woodland species

Visit Website

Awbury Arboretum

Address: 1 Awbury Rd, Philadelphia, PA 19138

Awbury Arboretum in Germantown is a hidden gem for Philadelphia birders. The 55-acre historic landscape features open meadows, mature trees, and a pond that attracts a variety of species. It is an especially welcoming space for beginner birders, as it is accessible and easy to navigate. Awbury has hosted birding programming as part of its broader environmental education mission.

Best for: Beginners, meadow and woodland species, a quieter alternative to larger parks

Visit Website

Fairmount Park

Address: Multiple accessible locations across Philadelphia

Fairmount Park is one of the largest urban park systems in the United States, encompassing thousands of acres across Philadelphia. Its varied habitats: woodland, meadow, and rivers support a wide range of species throughout the year. The Fairmount Park Discovery Center is a good resource for birders exploring the park system.

Best for: Year-round birding, varied habitats, accessible from many neighborhoods
