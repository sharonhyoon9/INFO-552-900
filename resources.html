Resources

A collection of recommended tools, books, websites, and other resources for beginner Birders.

Mobile Apps

  • Merlin Bird ID: Free. Created by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Merlin is the most recommended app for beginner birders. Identify birds by photo, by sound, or by answering a few simple questions. The Sound ID feature can identify birds from live audio in real time. Also works offline!
  • eBird Mobile: Free. Log your sightings, explore what birds have been seen recently in your area, and contribute to the world's largest citizen science database.
  • Audubon Bird Guide: Free. A comprehensive field guide app from the National Audubon Society covering nearly 800 North American species, with photos, range maps, and bird calls.
  • iNaturalist: Free. A broader nature observation platform useful for identifying birds and other wildlife. Observations can be confirmed by the community.

Books

  • The Sibley Guide to Birds by David Allen Sibley: Comprehensive coverage of over 900 North American species with detailed illustrations and range maps. A standard reference for birders at all levels.
  • Birds of Pennsylvania Field Guide by Stan Tekiela: A state-specific reference covering all species recorded in Pennsylvania, with images and notes.

Websites

  • All About Birds: Cornell Lab of Ornithology's free online bird guide. Detailed species accounts, photos, sounds, range maps, and life history information for every North American bird.
  • eBird Philadelphia County: Real-time sightings data, hotspot maps, and recent checklists for Philadelphia County. Essential for finding out what birds are being seen and where.
  • Delaware Valley Ornithological Club: Publications, and resources from one of the oldest ornithological organizations in the United States.
  • Pennsylvania Society for Ornithology: An annotated list of Pennsylvania Birds.

Films & Documentaries

  • A Glimpse Into Extreme Birdwatching (2011): An indie documentary that inspired this community website!
